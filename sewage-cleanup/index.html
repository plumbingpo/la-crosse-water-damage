Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across La Crosse, La Crosse Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else

How We Handle Sewage Cleanup

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Hazard Check and Containment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

Standing sewage is pumped out completely. Carpet and its padding, insulation, drywall and other contaminated porous items are removed, then disposed of properly as biohazard waste.

3

Cleaning and Disinfecting

EPA-registered antimicrobials are used to disinfect what is left after a thorough cleaning of each surface. HEPA air scrubbers remove airborne pathogens and contaminants.

4

Drying & Odor Elimination

Residual moisture is taken out with commercial-grade dryers. An industrial deodorizing treatment gets rid of the sewage smell. The space is confirmed safe to occupy by clearance testing.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
